Keep your Roblox account safe: cookies, scams and what real tools never ask for

By Deni · Published 25 October 2026 · 5 min read

Most Roblox accounts are not hacked. They are handed over, by the owner, to a page or a person who asked nicely. Tools that claim to check alts, give free Robux or "verify" you are a favourite disguise. Here is how these scams work, what a legitimate tool will and will not ask for, and the settings that make an account hard to lose.

Your cookie is your password

When you log in to Roblox, your browser receives a cookie called .ROBLOSECURITY. It is a long string of characters, and for as long as it is valid it is your login: anyone who has it can open your account without your password, without your email, and without triggering a login notification, because to Roblox it looks like you. That is what makes it the most valuable thing a scammer can get from you, and why every serious scam ends with a request for it, however it is dressed up.

The dressing varies. "Paste this into the console to claim your reward." "Our checker needs your cookie to see private data." "Send a screenshot of your cookies tab so we can verify you." "Install this extension." Each of these is a request to hand over your session. There is no legitimate reason for any website, Discord bot, game, friend or "Roblox staff member" to see your cookie. Ever.

How fake alt checkers work

Alt checking is a real need, so fake alt checkers are common. The honest ones read public profile data, which requires no login at all, and show you the result. The fake ones tell you they need you to sign in, or need your cookie, to "see hidden information" or "verify you are not a bot". Some then show you a plausible result, so you do not notice anything is wrong until your limiteds are gone and your password has been changed.

The tell is simple: a public-data check does not need your login. Everything an alt checker can legitimately see, you can see yourself by opening the profile while logged out. If a tool asks you to log in with Roblox, asks for a cookie, asks you to run a script, or asks you to install something, it wants your account, not the other person's. Close the tab.

The checker on this site follows this rule to the letter. You type the username you want to look at. The site has its own optional account system, with an email address and a password you choose for this site, so that limits can be applied and Pro can be switched on. It never asks for your Roblox credentials and could not use them if it had them.

The other classics

Free Robux generators
There is no way to generate Robux. Every site that says otherwise ends in a login form, a cookie request, a "human verification" survey that harvests your details, or malware.
Fake login pages
A link in chat or a Discord message leads to a page that looks like Roblox's login. The address is not roblox.com. Check the address bar before typing a password anywhere, and type roblox.com yourself rather than following links.
"Roblox staff" in chat
Roblox staff do not contact players through in-game chat or Discord, and never ask for passwords or codes. Anyone claiming otherwise is not staff.
Trade and item scams
"Send first and I'll send back", "trust trades", off-platform deals, and fake middlemen. Only use Roblox's own trade window, where both sides are shown before either confirms.
Account sales
Buying an account is against Roblox's rules, and the seller usually keeps the email and recovers it later. Old accounts are sold because age cannot be faked, which is the same reason they are reclaimed.
Beamers
Scammers who target accounts with valuable limiteds specifically, often through a cookie grab, then move the items out through a chain of alts within minutes. If you hold valuable items, every rule here applies twice.

Settings that actually protect you

  • Two-step verification. Turn it on in Settings under Security. An authenticator app is stronger than email. With it on, a stolen password alone is not enough to log in, and Roblox can require it before trades and before items leave the account.
  • Account PIN. A four-digit PIN that must be entered before settings can be changed. It stops someone who gets in from changing your email and locking you out.
  • A verified email you control. Recovery goes through it. If the email on the account is one you no longer have access to, fix that today.
  • A unique password. Most account takeovers start with a password reused from another site that leaked. A password manager makes this painless.
  • Log out everywhere if you think your cookie has been exposed. Settings has a "Sign out of all other sessions" option, which invalidates every existing cookie. Then change your password.
  • Privacy settings for younger players. Parental controls can restrict who can message, trade and send friend requests. A scam that cannot reach you cannot work.

If it already happened

Act in this order. Sign out of all sessions and change your password, from a device you trust. Turn on two-step verification. Check that the email and phone on the account are still yours. Then contact Roblox Support through roblox.com, not through anyone who messaged you offering to help; "recovery services" are a second scam aimed at people who just fell for a first one. Report the account that scammed you using the Report Abuse button on its profile.

What a legitimate tool looks like

It works while you are logged out of Roblox. It asks for the other account's name, not yours. It shows you where its information comes from, and the information matches what you see on the public profile. It does not need a browser extension, a script, a download or a Discord bot with permissions. If it has its own accounts, they use an email and password you chose for it, and it tells you plainly what it stores. Anything else is asking for more than it needs, and the only reason to ask for more than you need is to take it.
