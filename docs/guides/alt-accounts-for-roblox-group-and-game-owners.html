Alt accounts for Roblox group and game owners: ban evasion and fair moderation

By Deni · Published 25 October 2026 · 5 min read

If you run a Roblox group, a game, a clan or a community server, alts are a fact of life: the player you banned on Tuesday is back on Wednesday with a new name. This guide covers the tools Roblox actually gives you, the ones it does not, and how to write rules that stop ban evaders without driving away the new players you want.

The shape of the problem

Roblox accounts are free and take a minute to create, so a ban only removes one account, not one person. For a game or group of any size, some fraction of bans will be followed by an alt. Most ban evaders are lazy: the alt is brand new, has a default avatar, no friends, and a name that is the old one with digits on the end. A minority are careful and will dress the account up first. Your rules should catch the lazy majority cheaply and leave the careful minority to be caught by behaviour.

Inside a game: use the ban API

Since 2024, Roblox has given developers a built-in ban system through the Players service. A ban made this way is stored by Roblox, survives server restarts, can carry a duration and a message the banned player sees, and, crucially, has an option to extend the ban to the player's alternate accounts. Roblox decides what counts as an alternate account using signals only it has, such as device and network information. No public tool can do that, so if your problem is ban evasion inside your own game, this is the first thing to turn on. It will not catch everyone, but it catches the people a profile check also catches, and it does it automatically.

Pair it with an account-age gate if the problem is severe: require accounts to be a few days old before they can chat, trade or join ranked servers. The AccountAge property of a player is available to every game and costs nothing to check. Set the threshold low, a day or three, and be upfront about it in the game description, because every new player hits it.

In a group: you have less to work with

Groups have no ban API and no alt detection. You can kick and you can set join requirements, and that is roughly it. What works in practice:

  • Manual approval for joining, with a quick look at each profile. The 12-point checklist takes about a minute per account once you are used to it, and a tool like the checker does it in a few seconds with the reasoning shown.
  • An age rule for roles, not for joining. Let anyone join, but hold ranks that carry permissions until the account is a month old and has been active in the group.
  • Watch names. The most reliable alt tell in a group is a new member whose username is a banned member's name with a suffix, or whose display name matches a banned member exactly. Keep a list of banned names.
  • Look at who they are friends with. A new account whose only friends are the banned player's friends, or whose friends were all created the same week, is telling you something. The deep check on this site draws exactly that picture.

In a Discord or community server

Verification bots that link a Discord account to a Roblox account give you the Roblox username and ID, which is enough to run every check in this guide. Many of them can also enforce a minimum Roblox account age at verification time. Set one. It is the single cheapest filter you have, and it moves the cost of evasion from "make a new account" to "make a new account and wait".

Writing rules that are fair

The trap is to write "no alts". You cannot enforce it, because you cannot see who owns what, and it punishes the new player and the parent playing alongside their child while barely inconveniencing the evader, who will simply deny it. Write rules about what you can see and what people do:

  • New accounts have limits. State the age threshold and what it unlocks. This is a rule about a visible fact, applies to everyone equally, and needs no accusation.
  • Ban evasion is a ban. If a banned player returns on another account and you can show the link, the new account is banned for evasion, not for being an alt. The link is what you have to show: matching name, same friends, same behaviour, or a confession.
  • Behaviour decides. A new account that follows the rules stays. A five-year-old account that breaks them goes. Profile signals decide how closely you watch, never what you do.

Using an alt score responsibly

A score from a profile check is a reason to look, not a finding. Some ground rules that keep it that way:

  • Never ban or kick on a score alone. Use it to decide which new members get a second look.
  • Never post someone's score in public or tell them they "scored as an alt". It is an estimate from counts, it can be wrong, and saying it out loud to a genuine new player costs you that player.
  • Read the signals, not the number. "Created three days ago, no friends, name is BannedGuy_2" is a case. "Score 74" is not.
  • Give young accounts time. The honest reading of a one-week-old account is "too early to tell", and your rules should let it earn trust over the following weeks.
  • Keep a record of why you acted, in a mod log. If the decision is questioned later, "profile matched a banned member's name and friends" holds up; "the tool said 80" does not.

What this will not fix

A determined person with time will beat every profile check: they will age an account for a month, add real friends, buy a hat, farm nothing, and come back quietly. No public tool catches that, and you should not promise your community one that does. What profile checks do is make evasion cost effort instead of a minute, and most people who get banned are not willing to spend the effort. That is a real win. For the few who are, the only answer is the same one it has always been: watch what they do, and act on that.
