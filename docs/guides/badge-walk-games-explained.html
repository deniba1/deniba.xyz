Badge walks and badge farming on Roblox, explained

By Deni · Published 25 October 2026 · 4 min read

Game badges are one of the best signs that a Roblox account has actually been played. They are also the easiest sign to fake, thanks to a whole genre of games that exist to hand badges out. If you judge accounts by their badges, you need to know how badge walks work and how to see through them.

What a game badge is

Any Roblox game can create badges and award them to players for whatever the developer decides: finishing a level, finding a hidden room, playing for an hour, visiting on launch day. Badges appear on the player's profile under Player Badges, with the game they came from and the date they were earned. Roblox charges developers a small fee per badge created beyond a free daily allowance, which keeps the number of badges per game sane but does nothing to stop a game from awarding its badges to everyone who walks in.

For most of Roblox's history, a profile with hundreds of badges from dozens of games was an account that had been played for years. That is still true of most such profiles, and it is why badge count is a useful signal.

What a badge walk is

A badge walk, also called a badge farm or free badge game, is a game whose entire content is badges. You spawn in a long corridor or an open field dotted with pads. Each pad you step on awards a badge. Some have a few dozen; the larger ones have hundreds, and the player follows a marked route that collects them all in ten or fifteen minutes. There are also obby-style ones where each checkpoint is a badge, and "tycoon" ones where each purchase awards one.

These games are not against Roblox's rules, and there is a whole community of badge collectors who enjoy them for their own sake, competing over who has the highest badge count. The problem is only what they do to profile checks.

How they distort a profile

A brand-new account can walk through three badge games in an evening and come out with five hundred badges. On paper it now looks like a veteran. Anyone, or any tool, that reads "500 badges" as "500 badges' worth of playing" is fooled. People who farm alts for ban evasion, botting or scams know this, and badge-walking a fresh account is a standard way to make it look lived in. The same goes for accounts being sold: a padded badge count makes an empty account look more valuable.

Telling farmed badges from earned ones

The badges themselves are indistinguishable. The timing is not. Roblox records when each badge was awarded, and the record is public. Real play produces badges spread out over days, weeks and years, one or two per session, from games the player comes back to. Badge walking produces a wall of badges with timestamps seconds apart, all from the same game, and then nothing.

The practical test used by the checker on this site: group the badges by when they were earned, and treat any run of ten or more badges within ten minutes as a burst. A single burst can happen in a legitimate game that awards several badges at once, so a burst or two is ignored. When bursts account for twenty or more badges and half or more of the total, the account is flagged as badge-farmed, and only the badges earned at a normal pace count toward "this account gets played". A separate signal rewards badges spread across fifteen or more different days, which no badge walk produces.

By hand, the same thing: open the badge list, sort by date, and look for dozens of badges sharing one date. Click one and look at the game it came from. If the title says "FREE BADGES", "Badge Walk" or "1000 Badges", you have your answer.

The last row matters. Badge collectors trip the burst test constantly and are entirely genuine, which is why the days-with-a-badge count is the tiebreaker. Bursts across four hundred different days is a hobby. Bursts on one day is a disguise.

Roblox badges are a different thing

Do not confuse game badges with Roblox badges, the small set awarded by Roblox itself and shown separately on the profile: Friendship, Homestead, Bricksmith, Welcome To The Club, Veteran and a handful of others. These cannot be farmed in a badge walk. They come from making friends, having people visit a place you built, holding Premium, or being on the platform for a year. An account with none of them has done very little, whatever its game badge count says.

Reading the player badge check

Roblox's public badge endpoints are rate-limited, and reading the timestamps of several hundred badges takes a number of requests. The checker on this site reads them when it can; when Roblox refuses, the result says so under "Not checked" rather than guessing, and you can re-run the lookup a minute later. If badges matter to your decision and the check was skipped, wait for it rather than acting on the rest.

The short version

  • Badges prove play only if they were earned over time.
  • Dozens of badges with the same date from one game are a badge walk.
  • Count the days badges were earned on, not the badges.
  • Roblox's own badges cannot be farmed and are worth more per badge than any game's.
