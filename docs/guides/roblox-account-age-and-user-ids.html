Roblox account age: join dates, user IDs and what they reveal

By Deni · Published 25 October 2026 · 4 min read

Account age is the strongest single clue to whether a Roblox account is a throwaway, and it is also the hardest to fake: nobody can make an account older. Here is where to find it, what to make of it, and how a bare user ID gives you a rough creation date even when the profile tells you nothing.

Where the join date is

Open any profile and look in the About section. Roblox shows a Join Date there, with the exact day. Roblox also exposes the creation timestamp through its public user API, which is what tools read; both come from the same record and agree with each other.

The join date is the one thing on a profile that the owner cannot change, hide or buy. Friends can be added, items bought, badges farmed, descriptions written. The date stays.

How to read an age

AgeWhat it usually means
Under a weekToo new to judge. If the account is already breaking rules, that is behaviour, not age.
1–4 weeksStill very young. Look at whether the profile is filling in: friends, favorites, a changed avatar.
1–3 monthsA real player's profile has usually picked up a dozen badges and some friends by now.
3–12 monthsSettled. Sparse at this age is unusual but happens with casual players.
1–3 yearsEstablished. Alts this old exist, but they are rarely throwaways.
3 years and upLong-standing. Treat as a main unless something specific says otherwise.

Age interacts with everything else. A one-month-old account with no friends is unremarkable. A five-year-old account with no friends, no badges and no favorites is odd: someone made it and never used it, which fits a storage alt, a forgotten first account, or an account bought from someone else.

User IDs are a clock

Every Roblox account has a numeric user ID, visible in the profile URL: roblox.com/users/156/profile is user 156. IDs are handed out in order as accounts are created. User 1 is Roblox itself, user 156 is builderman, and the account made a second ago has an ID just past the previous record. That means the ID alone tells you roughly when an account was created, because every ID falls between two points whose dates are known.

Between two rows, assume the IDs were handed out evenly. An ID of 1.5 billion sits halfway between March 2019 and November 2020, so it was created around early 2020. The estimate is usually within a few weeks for recent accounts and within a few months for old ones, because the rate of sign-ups has changed so much: it took Roblox nine years to reach 100 million accounts and about one more year to reach the next 100 million, and recently a billion IDs go by in well under a year.

Why this matters when the profile is hidden

The join date is on the profile, but you do not always have the profile. A friends list, a group member list, a server log or a Discord verification bot may give you only names and IDs. The ID lets you sort a list of fifty accounts into "years old" and "made last week" without opening fifty pages. That is how the deep check on this site judges an account's friends: it estimates every friend's age from their ID in one pass, then opens only a spread of them for a closer look.

The ID also catches a specific pattern: a cluster of accounts with IDs a few hundred apart, meaning they were created within minutes of each other. Six friends with near-consecutive IDs were made in one sitting, by one person or one script.

Things that look like age but are not

  • The Veteran badge. Roblox awards it after a year on the platform. Its presence confirms the account is at least a year old; its absence on an older account just means the badge was not collected.
  • Past usernames. A long list suggests the account has been around, but each change costs Robux and can happen at any time; it is a sign of investment, not of age.
  • Old-style usernames. Short, dictionary-word names were only available early on, so a name like Falcon is almost certainly old. A name with a long run of digits is probably recent, but not certainly: digit names have been given out since the beginning.
  • Bought accounts. Old accounts are sold, against Roblox's rules, precisely because age cannot be faked. A 2012 account that went dormant for years and suddenly behaves like a brand-new troll may have changed hands. Age says the account is old; it does not say who is at the keyboard.

What age cannot tell you

Age says how long the account has existed, not how it has been used and not who owns it. A one-week-old account belongs to a brand-new player far more often than to a ban evader, because Roblox gains far more new players than it bans. Age should decide how much the rest of the profile has to prove, not stand in for it. The rest of the profile is covered in the 12-point checklist.
